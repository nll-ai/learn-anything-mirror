Privacy notice — Sage, the Learn Anything community agent

Effective date: October 8, 2026

Who we are. Learn Anything is a Discord community operated by Non-Linear Labs, LLC (“Nonlinear Labs”) (Eric Ma and Daniel). The Discord application and bot are operated by Eric Ma on the community’s behalf. The contact for anything in this notice is eric.ma@nonlinearlabs.ai.

What Sage is. Sage is an AI assistant the community runs to help welcome members, answer questions, and keep the server safe. Sage is rolled out in stages: it observes and records server activity, and in channels where we’ve turned answering on, it also answers members who @mention it. This notice explains what Sage records and how that data is handled.

What Sage records

Regardless of mode, Sage records activity metadata: event types (messages, reactions, joins, AutoMod executions), server/channel/message/author IDs, timestamps, reaction emoji, attachment counts, and member details (username, display name, join time, and when Sage last saw you active).

When content capture is on, Sage additionally stores message excerpts capped at 500 characters and text that server AutoMod rules matched. Content capture is off right now. If we turn it on later, we’ll update this notice and announce it in the server before enabling it.

When you @mention Sage a question, Sage also keeps a session record of how it produced the answer: your question word for word, your username and ID, the short conversation context it worked from (described under “Other services” below), and the results of the searches it ran — which can include other members’ messages from the channels Sage operates in, from people who never interacted with Sage. The community operators use these records to check Sage’s work and fix mistakes. They are stored as files in the same United States Modal storage described below, and only the community operators have routine access to them. If a member featured in someone’s session record asks us, we’ll delete their parts of it too.

Sage never stores direct-message content (it doesn’t process DMs at all) and never stores the option values you type into slash commands.

When you @mention Sage a question, Sage also keeps a short answer-audit record: who asked, which channel, when, whether it could answer from its community notes, and which safety filters ran. That audit record is metadata only — the question’s text lives in the session record described above, whether or not content capture is on.

When you @mention Sage in a channel where answering is on and you explicitly offer a fact, ritual, resource, or correction for Sage’s notes, Sage may file that offer as a private GitHub issue for Eric and Daniel to review. Ordinary questions do not file. “Add this” with no fact does not file. Ambient capture of channel chat into the notes stays off. There is no magic prefix or separate submit command. The GitHub write happens when Sage files the issue; the Discord receipt that follows is notice after the copy, not the moment of copy.

What is copied onto that issue: your proposed text (verbatim), Sage’s short judgment and (when produced) a landing sketch of how the facts could land in the notes, your Discord username and user id, and the message, channel, and server ids. Eric and Daniel, and anyone with access to the private nll-ai/learn-anything repository, can read it. The Discord receipt @-mentions Eric and Daniel so they can review.

If Eric or Daniel approve and merge the proposal, a page is added or updated under Sage’s private notes (sage/kb/) and the next Sage deploy (Modal) can answer from it. That page may be a short Sage-written version of your facts, not a silent drop of the verbatim source (the verbatim text stays on the issue). Discord username and user id stay on the GitHub issue, not in the baked answering file.

You keep the copyright. By submitting the offer, you license Nonlinear Labs to put these words on a private GitHub issue for Eric and Daniel to review, and if they merge it, to store your words and/or that short Sage-written version in Sage’s private notes and use them to answer this community. Don’t submit text you don’t have the right to share. Don’t put other people’s private facts, health, or IDs in a proposal. Founders strip that before approve.

Why

To run the community agent: onboarding, answering questions grounded in community knowledge, flagging possible rule-breaking for human review, and improving the answers Sage can give. Member history is used to inform human moderation only — never automated punishment.

What Sage doesn’t do

Sage never issues punishments on its own; it flags and logs, and people act. Sage always identifies itself as an AI when it answers you, and Discord labels Sage as a bot. Sage never sends direct messages about moderation decisions; people handle those end to end.

Where it lives

In a database hosted on Modal in the United States, encrypted at rest by Modal’s platform. Only the community operators (Eric Ma and Daniel) have routine access to it. Session records (described above) live as files in that same Modal storage, under the same access rules. Sage also writes brief operational logs to Modal’s dashboard — IDs, event types, and counts, never message content — for running and troubleshooting the service.

KB proposals also live on GitHub (Issues and pull requests on the private nll-ai/learn-anything repository). If merged, the notes page lives in that repository and in the next Sage deploy on Modal. GitHub is a data location, not only Modal.

How long we keep it

Activity-log entries are deleted after 90 days (culled daily). Answer-audit records and session records from @mentions follow the same 90-day window. Member records are kept while you’re in the server and until you ask us to delete them.

Approved KB pages, GitHub issues and pull requests, and git history are a different record class: they are curated knowledge, not session telemetry, and the 90-day window does not apply to them. If you ask Eric or Daniel to take a page out of Sage’s notes, Sage will delete or stub the page under sage/kb/ and the next deploy will stop answering from it. We do not rewrite git history, GitHub issue or pull-request bodies, Actions logs, prior container images, or the Discord thread. A copy we hand you is the markdown page plus its provenance fields (source Discord URL and issue number), not every git object.

Your choices

You can ask for a copy of your data, a correction, or deletion at any time: message Eric or Daniel in the server, or email eric.ma@nonlinearlabs.ai. Leaving the server does not automatically delete your activity entries or member record — ask us and we’ll remove them. Copies and deletions cover session records: we’ll hand you (or erase) the session files for your asks, including your parts of records that also mention others. For a notes page that came from a proposal, ask Eric or Daniel to take the page out of Sage’s notes as described above.

Sensitive information

Please don’t post sensitive personal information (health, finances, government IDs) in the server. If a message is flagged, a human reviews it.

Age

Sage operates in our Discord server, and membership follows Discord’s Terms of Service, including Discord’s minimum-age requirements. If you believe we hold data about someone under Discord’s minimum age, contact us and we’ll delete it.

Other services

Discord handles your Discord account and server data under its own privacy policy. When you @mention Sage a question, the question’s text is sent to our AI provider — z.ai, operated by Jingsheng Hengxing Technology Pte. Ltd. — to generate the answer. So Sage can follow the conversation you’re asking in, it also sends a short excerpt of that conversation as context: your recent exchange with Sage and, when you ask inside a reply thread, the thread’s earlier messages (other members’ messages included, shown with their Discord names). When Sage files a KB proposal in that same turn, the model also supplies its judgment and landing sketch as tool arguments; those arguments go to z.ai in the same answering turn. They are not sent there to rewrite your words off the issue: the verbatim text stays on the issue as source. Their terms say the content is processed in real time, not saved on their servers, and is not used to train their models; like most providers they keep limited exceptions for abuse monitoring and legal compliance. We recorded those terms before turning question-answering on. Their terms: terms of use (including the API additional terms) and privacy policy (which links their Data Processing Addendum).

If you’re in the EEA or UK

We process community data based on our legitimate interests in operating and keeping safe a community you chose to join. Sage may infer that a message is an explicit offer to add something to its notes; that is not a member-typed submit command, and this notice does not treat that path as a consent claim. You may ask for access, correction, deletion, or restriction of your data, object to our processing, or ask us to transfer it — using the contact above. You can also complain to your local data-protection supervisory authority (or the UK ICO). We keep your data only as long as described above.
